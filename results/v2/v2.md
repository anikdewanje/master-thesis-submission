## V2 -- handshake closure (agentic only, descriptive)

The control is one node with no peers: it issues no inter-agent requests and has no
closure denominator at all. This is not a score the two systems share.

| handshake | requests | closed | rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `capacity_query` | 570 | 540 | 0.947 |
| `dispatch_mission` | 365 | 354 | 0.970 |
| `confirm_dropoff` | 331 | 330 | 0.997 |
| `request_medical` | 277 | 253 | 0.913 |
| `dispatch_escort_mission` | 261 | 233 | 0.893 |
| `mission_complete` | 97 | 85 | 0.876 |
| `abort_request` | 31 | 17 | 0.548 |
| **all** | **1932** | **1812** | **0.938** |

No reply *latency* is reported. `sim_time` is each recipient's own clock and `ts` is a
per-turn flush stamp, so neither orders two agents' rows reliably; closure is therefore
defined as a bipartite matching and is stable under every ordering, while a lag computed
from those clocks would not be (`facts_v2.match_handshakes`).

Abort reasons, 108 aborts:

| reason | n | share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `failure_response` | 57 | 52.8% |
| `churn_initiated` | 14 | 13.0% |
| `other` | 37 | 34.3% |

> **Association, not causation.** Plan revisions track survival and the full-delivery
> rate falls monotonically across the bands, but the majority of aborts are explicit
> post-failure stand-downs, so a run that is going badly re-plans *because* it is going
> badly. Observational data cannot separate the two directions. The experiment that
> would is a commitment-constrained variant — cap re-plans, or forbid cancelling a leg
> already in flight (`thresholds_v2.CHURN_CAUSAL_DIRECTION_UNRESOLVED`).
